A practical guide

What is FMEA?

Failure Mode and Effects Analysis (FMEA) is a structured way to identify how a system could fail, understand the impact, and decide what to do before that failure happens.

How it works

  1. Describe the failure mode and its effect on the robot or team.
  2. Identify potential causes and the controls already in place.
  3. Rate the risk using severity, occurrence, and detection.
  4. Plan action items to reduce risk, then review and update the analysis.

Understanding the ratings

Severity (S)
How serious the worst credible consequence would be if the failure happened.
Occurrence (O)
How likely the failure is to happen in any one match.
Detection (D)
How likely the team's current checks are to miss the issue before it affects a match.

Rate failures in the context of an FRC competition event, not across a whole season. For match-related failures, estimate occurrence per match; the chance of seeing it at least once over an event will rise with the number of matches. Severity is the consequence, not the chance of occurrence. Detection is based on checks and safeguards the team currently uses. Higher detection scores mean those controls are less likely to catch the issue in time. These are practical guideposts, not a universal FMEA scale.

Severity (S)

Worst credible consequence if the failure happens during the event
ScoreMeaning
1No noticeable effect on match performance.
2Minor inconvenience; the robot performs as planned.
3Brief disruption, with no meaningful loss of function.
4Small performance reduction; the robot completes its planned role.
5Clear performance loss, but the robot still contributes.
6An important function is lost, limiting the robot's contribution.
7The robot cannot perform its planned role for a significant part of a match.
8The robot is disabled or provides little contribution for most of a match.
9The match outcome is likely affected, or substantial damage requires lengthy repair.
10A serious safety hazard occurs, or damage makes the robot unusable for the rest of the event.

Occurrence (O)

Approximate chance the failure happens in any one match
ScoreChance
1Less than 1% — exceptionally unlikely.
21% to less than 5% — very rare.
35% to less than 10% — rare.
410% to less than 20% — unlikely.
520% to less than 30% — occasional.
630% to less than 45% — plausible.
745% to less than 60% — fairly likely.
860% to less than 75% — likely.
975% to 90% — very likely.
10More than 90% — almost certain.

Detection (D)

Chance the team's current checks miss the issue before it affects a match
ScoreMeaning
1Automatic safeguards almost always detect or prevent it before match impact.
2A reliable built-in check catches it in nearly every case.
3Routine pre-match checks are very likely to catch it.
4Routine checks usually catch it, though misses are possible.
5A deliberate, targeted check is more likely than not to catch it.
6Checks catch it inconsistently; it may reach a match unnoticed.
7Routine checks usually miss it; a specific test is needed.
8It is unlikely to be caught without specialized testing or diagnostics.
9Existing checks almost never catch it before it affects a match.
10No known practical check catches it before it affects a match.

The Risk Priority Number (RPN) is calculated as S × O × D. It helps teams compare risks and prioritize follow-up; it is a guide for discussion, not a substitute for engineering judgment.

Keep it useful

Review the FMEA as the design, build, and testing progress. When an action is completed or new information becomes available, update the entry so the team has a current picture of its risks.
